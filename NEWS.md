## LEAD — US proposes new tariffs; Wall Street plunges, then rebounds
- The Trump administration on Wednesday proposed new Section 301 tariffs
  on 14 major trading partners after an eight-month investigation into
  structural excess manufacturing capacity. Highest rate: China 20%
  (on top of roughly 25% first-term duties; could reach 40% if talks
  fail). EU capped at 15% (could rise to 20%); Vietnam 20%; Cambodia and
  Indonesia 18%; India 17%; EU/Japan/Korea/Taiwan broadly capped at 15%.
  Others: Bangladesh, Malaysia, Singapore, Switzerland, Thailand.
  Public comment first; earliest effect January. The probe followed the
  Supreme Court striking down Trump's earlier sweeping global tariffs
  in February.
- Markets Wednesday: the Nasdaq Composite plunged as much as 4.6%
  intraday (low 22,632.40; Nasdaq 100 briefly -20% from its record
  close) as the AI selloff deepened and tariff news hit, then bargain
  hunters stepped in before the Thanksgiving holiday. Closes: Nasdaq
  -0.62% to 23,585.80; S&P 500 -0.25% to 7,182.78; Dow +0.33% to
  50,182.95. US markets are closed Thursday for Thanksgiving.
- Nvidia fell 3.91% to close at $149.30 — its third straight loss after
  Monday's +31.5% Huang-return rally and Tuesday's -5.9%. Morning
  reports had it down as much as ~8.3% intraday. October core PCE
  inflation came in at 3.3% vs 3.2% forecast, adding pressure.
- Sources: Reuters 25 Nov (tariff proposal; closing wrap); Bloomberg via
  SwissInfo 25 Nov; CNN 25 Nov; Al Jazeera 25 Nov; AP via TVNZ 25 Nov;
  FinanceFeeds 26 Nov (intraday low/close table, CNBC data); Finnhub

## GRID 1 — HORMUZ: tankers hit mines; Houthis claim refinery attack
- Iran said on Wednesday that two oil tankers struck naval mines and
  exploded/caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz after taking an
  unauthorised route; a further tanker hit a mine in a "restricted
  zone" east of the strait the same day (Iranian Army via Fars).
  No vessel names, flags or casualty counts were given and no
  independent maritime agency had confirmed the strikes by Wednesday
  afternoon — the image therefore attributes the claim to Iran.
- Yemen's Houthis said they attacked Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura oil
  refinery (550,000 bbl/day, ~16% of Saudi refining capacity, plus a
  major export terminal) and Riyadh's financial district, claiming
  fires. The Saudi-led coalition said air defences intercepted four
  drones over Ras Tanura; satellite imagery showed black smoke over
  the complex.
- Brent crude finished Wednesday +3.5%; WTI +2% on supply concerns.
- Sources: Reuters 26 Nov (early-Asia wrap of Wednesday's session);
  TML News 25 Nov; Times Now live blog 25–26 Nov; Just Security Early
  Edition 25 Nov.

## GRID 2 — SPACE: first foreign astronaut aboard China's Tiangong
- Shenzhou-24 launched at 08:18 local time (0018 GMT) Wednesday from
  the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Centre on a Long March-2F rocket.
  Crew: commander Tang Hongbo, flight engineer Wu Yi, and Pakistani
  payload specialist Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, 40, a Pakistan Air Force
  pilot — Pakistan's first astronaut and the first foreign crew
  member aboard China's Tiangong space station.
- The spacecraft entered orbit about 10 minutes after launch and
  docked with Tiangong about 3.5 hours later (11:56 Beijing time).
  Ali will stay roughly a week (reports range 5–10 days), running
  experiments including Pakistani university biotechnology payloads,
  then return with two of the outgoing Shenzhou-23 crew.
- Sources: AP via Military.com 25 Nov; Reuters via Al Jazeera 25 Nov;
  CNA 25 Nov; Nature 24–25 Nov; SpacePolicyOnline 25 Nov.

## GRID 3 — HUAWEI convicted in the US; China condemns the verdict
- A Brooklyn federal jury convicted Huawei and three subsidiaries on
  Tuesday on 10 of 12 counts (some reports say 10 of 11 for the parent
  — the dossier uses 10 of 12, the DOJ/Reuters formulation): bank
  fraud, wire fraud, money-laundering conspiracy, conspiracy to steal
  trade secrets, and Iran-sanctions (IEEPA) violations. Acquitted on
  racketeering conspiracy and obstruction-of-justice conspiracy.
  The trial lasted about three months; the case began in 2018.
- On Wednesday China lashed out, accusing the US of "suppressing"
  Chinese companies; Huawei called the conviction "without legal or
  factual merit" and said it will appeal. Sentencing is set for
  14 September 2027 (fines, forfeiture, restitution possible).
- Sources: CNN 24–25 Nov; WSJ 25 Nov; Fox Business/DOJ 25 Nov;
  Al Jazeera 25 Nov; The Daily Star 25–26 Nov (China reaction).

## GRID 4 — UKRAINE strikes a major Russian air base
- Ukraine's General Staff said on Wednesday its forces struck the
  Savasleyka air base in Russia's Nizhny Novgorod region, east of
  Moscow, damaging five aircraft used to launch missiles and bombs:
  three MiG-31 jets (hypersonic-missile carriers) and two Su-34
  fighter-bombers. Russia did not immediately comment.
- Also on 25 Nov: a large-scale Russian strike forced a DTEK thermal
  power plant to suspend generation as Russia intensified attacks on
  Ukraine's energy and heating infrastructure ahead of winter
  (Hudson Institute situation report, 25 Nov). Ukraine's General
  Staff reported 151 combat engagements on 25 Nov, 47 Russian
  airstrikes and 3,870 kamikaze drones (Ukrinform, as of 22:00).
  Overnight Russian strikes on the Odesa region the night before
  killed two people, including a 17-year-old boy.
- Sources: Reuters 25 Nov; Al Jazeera 26 Nov (report of Wednesday's
  General Staff statement); Hudson Institute 25 Nov; Ukrinform 25 Nov.
